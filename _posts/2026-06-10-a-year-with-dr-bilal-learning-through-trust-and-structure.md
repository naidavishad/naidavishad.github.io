My entire first year was spent under the supervision of Dr. Bilal, and looking back, I feel genuinely grateful for that experience.

Sir's approach to teaching and supervision was one that gave students space. He trusted us to take ownership of our work, to manage our time, and to figure things out on our own. That kind of autonomy is rare, and for someone who learns best by doing, it was deeply valuable. It pushed me to be more self-directed, more proactive, and more honest with myself about where I was and where I needed to be.

At the same time, Sir was not hands-off to the point of being absent. When he sensed that students were drifting or losing focus, he stepped in — calmly but firmly — and set deadlines that brought everyone back on track. It was a balance that worked well. The freedom to grow, and the structure to keep from getting lost.

All in all, the year spent under his guidance was one I look back on with appreciation. He created an environment where both learning and accountability could coexist, and that shaped how I approach my work even now.
