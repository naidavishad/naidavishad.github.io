In Digital Logic Design, my group and I took on a project that was as challenging as it was exciting — a Fastest Finger Game, a circuit-based system designed to detect and display which player pressed their button first.

The concept was straightforward enough, but the execution was anything but. We ran into significant issues during the circuit implementation phase. Connections that should have worked did not, outputs were inconsistent, and there were moments where the whole thing felt too far from working to fix in time. But we did not give up. We sat with the problem, tested, revised, and pushed through — and eventually, we got it working.

The DLD Exhibition was the first of the semester, and presenting a project that we had genuinely struggled with made the moment of completion feel all the more satisfying. It was a reminder that the projects worth being proud of are rarely the ones that came easily.
