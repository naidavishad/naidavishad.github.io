When I first joined university, I stepped into the world of student societies as a general member — curious, observant, and eager to find my place. I attended events, supported tasks wherever I could, and slowly began to understand how these organizations functioned behind the scenes. It was a quieter role, but an important one. It gave me the foundation I needed.

By the second semester, that foundation paid off. I was selected as the Webmaster of IEEE CIS (Computational Intelligence Society) — a role that came with real responsibility and real visibility. It was not just a title. It meant representing the society, managing its digital presence, and being part of a team that was working toward something meaningful. When I found out, late at night, I remember feeling genuinely overwhelmed with happiness. It was one of those moments that reminded me why showing up — even when it feels small — always matters.
