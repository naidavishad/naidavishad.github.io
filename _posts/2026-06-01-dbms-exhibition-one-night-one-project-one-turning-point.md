Of all the moments this semester, the night before the Database Management Systems (DBMS) Exhibition is the one I will remember most clearly.

I had one night. That was all the time I had left. And every part of the project — the database design, the model training, the final polish — still needed to be done. I sat down and started working, determined to see it through. Hour after hour passed. The work was slow, and the night felt endless.

Somewhere in those early morning hours, the exhaustion and the pressure reached a point I could not hold back anymore. I broke down and cried. It was not a moment of weakness — it was simply a moment of being human. The weight of the semester, the sleepless nights, the pressure of performance — it all surfaced at once.

But I did not stop. I wiped my tears, took a breath, and went back to work. By the time the sun came up, the database was fully designed and the project was ready for submission.

That night changed something in me. It taught me, in the most personal way possible, that resilience is not about never struggling. It is about choosing to continue — even when you are running on nothing but will. I walked into that exhibition not just with a project, but with a story of what it took to get there.
