The first day at university was one of the most important and unforgettable days of my life. As a girl coming from another city, everything was completely new for me. I had recently left my home, my family, and my comfortable life to start a new journey. Although I was excited to begin my university education, I also felt nervous because I did not know anyone, and I had never lived away from my family before.

When I entered the university campus for the first time, I was amazed by its size and beauty. The campus was much larger than my school, with different academic blocks, laboratories, libraries, gardens, cafeterias, sports grounds, and hostels. I saw many students walking around, talking with their friends, and finding their classrooms. Since I was new, I felt a little confused about where to go and how everything worked. Fortunately, the senior students and university staff guided us politely and helped us find our classrooms.

As I walked towards my classroom, I noticed many students who looked just as nervous as I was. Some had come from different cities, while others were from nearby areas. Everyone was trying to make new friends and adjust to the new environment. Slowly, I introduced myself to a few classmates, and we started talking about our hometowns, interests, and the departments we had joined. Those simple conversations helped reduce my nervousness, and I realized that everyone was facing similar feelings.

The first few weeks of the semester were dedicated to introductory lectures. During these sessions, our teachers introduced themselves, shared their academic backgrounds, and explained the subjects they would teach. They discussed the course outlines, grading system, attendance policy, assignments, quizzes, presentations, laboratory work, and examinations. They also explained the expectations they had from students and encouraged us to participate actively in class. These introductory lectures helped us understand that university education is very different from school education.

One of the biggest differences I noticed was that university students are expected to take responsibility for their own learning. In school, teachers usually reminded us about every homework assignment and guided us through every lesson. At university, we had to manage our own time, complete assignments before deadlines, prepare for quizzes, and study independently. At first, this felt challenging, but I gradually understood that becoming independent is an important part of university life.

Since I had come from another city, adjusting to the new environment was not always easy. I missed my parents, my siblings, and the comfort of home. There were moments when I felt homesick, especially during the first few days. Everything around me was unfamiliar, including the hostel, the classrooms, and even the daily routine. However, I reminded myself that every new beginning comes with challenges, and I decided to stay positive and make the most of this opportunity.

As the days passed, I became more familiar with the university campus. I learned where the library, laboratories, cafeteria, and different departments were located. Walking around the campus every day helped me feel more comfortable. I also discovered quiet places where I could study and relax between classes. Slowly, the university started feeling like my second home.

Meeting new classmates was another memorable part of my first days. My classmates came from different cities, cultures, and educational backgrounds. Everyone had unique experiences and different dreams for the future. Working together during class activities helped us become friends. We studied together, shared notes, discussed assignments, and supported each other whenever someone needed help. These friendships made the transition to university life much easier.

The first visit to the university laboratories was another exciting experience. Everything looked modern and professional. The computer labs were equipped with many computers, while the science laboratories contained different instruments and equipment. Entering these labs made me realize that university learning is not only about reading books but also about gaining practical experience. I was excited because I knew that I would have the opportunity to learn many new skills through practical work.

The teachers also encouraged us to participate in classroom discussions and ask questions whenever we did not understand something. At first, I was shy because I was afraid of making mistakes. However, I soon realized that the teachers appreciated students who were curious and willing to learn. This friendly atmosphere helped me become more confident in speaking and participating during lectures.

During the first few weeks, I also learned how important time management is in university life. We had different subjects, laboratory sessions, assignments, quizzes, and presentations. Managing everything required planning and discipline. I started using a daily schedule to organize my classes, study time, and personal activities. This habit helped me complete my work on time and reduced my stress.

Living away from home also taught me many life lessons. I became more independent and responsible because I had to manage my own daily routine. I learned how to balance my academic responsibilities with my personal life. Although I missed my family, regular phone calls with them gave me motivation and emotional support. Their encouragement reminded me why I had come to university and inspired me to work hard.

As the weeks passed, my confidence continued to grow. The fear I had on my first day slowly disappeared. I became familiar with my teachers, classmates, classrooms, and university environment. Every lecture taught me something new, every assignment improved my skills, and every conversation helped me build stronger friendships. The university no longer felt like a strange place. Instead, it became a place where I could learn, grow, and prepare for my future.

Looking back, my first days at university were full of excitement, nervousness, curiosity, and learning. Leaving my city and moving to a new place was not easy, but it helped me become stronger, more confident, and more independent. Those early days taught me how to adapt to a new environment, make new friends, manage responsibilities, and face challenges with courage. They marked the beginning of an unforgettable journey that continues to shape both my academic life and my personal growth. Even today, whenever I remember my first days at university, I feel proud of myself for taking that important step toward achieving my dreams.
